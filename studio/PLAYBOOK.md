# Challenge-video playbook (studied from the MrBeast format)

Distilled from public reporting on the leaked "How to Succeed in MrBeast Production" guide
and from the titles of the channel's most-viewed videos. Every point maps to something
in `everyday/episodes/last_to_leave_circle.py`.

## Topic selection
- One sentence anyone understands instantly: **simple rule + huge stake**.
- Proven frames: *Last To Leave X Wins $*, *$1 vs $1,000,000 X*, *Ages 1–100 Compete*,
  *100 People vs 1*, *Survive X Days In Y*. ("Last To Leave Circle Wins $500,000" is among the
  channel's most-viewed videos ever.)
- Built-in escalation: the format itself must get harder every few minutes.

## Title
- Under ~50 characters, reads like a headline, no clickbait the video doesn't pay off.
- Big round number + clear rule + exclamation. Title and thumbnail say **different** things.

## Thumbnail
- One focal subject with a readable emotion; ≤3 words; huge contrast (orange lava vs cyan gems).
- Must read at 168 px wide (check `thumbnail_size_test.png`).
- Ship 3 variants to YouTube **Test & Compare** — let real viewers pick.

## First minute (most viewers leave here)
- No intro. First line = the title promise, on screen, immediately.
- Preview the escalations (shrink → lava → bribes) so viewers know it gets bigger.

## Keeping them to the end
- A visible counter that only goes one way (PLAYERS LEFT).
- A new round/twist every 20–40 s; banners make each round feel like a new episode.
- Open loops before the usual mid-video dip ("round four is where it gets crazy").
- Characters with one clear trait each (the underdog, the bully, the smart one).
- Plant early, pay off late (Zara's snacks → Pip shares the prize).
- Escalating final offer (50k → 100k → 250k → 500k), then a generous ending.
- End screen teases the next video by name.
